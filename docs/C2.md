## Results (all full runs: C2 is CPU-only and small)

**Sources.** Project Gutenberg, `topic=poetry`, English, public domain, first 40 books after a title filter. The filter removes prose translations, collected plays and book-length epics ("Iliad", "Complete Works", …). 36 books survive cleaning. Every record has `id`, `source`, `book`, `book_author`, `licence`, `title`, `author`, `text`.

**Task 1: the funnel.**

Most words go to `prose` and `max_lines`. Both were inspected (20 removals each):
- **`max_lines`** removes prose blocks and epic cantos (Ramayana, Lucretius, Homeric hymns), but also real long poems ("Divided", Burns's "Epistle to James Smith"). It is kept on purpose: a poem that does not fit D1's window gets truncated or dropped later anyway. The rule is a modelling choice, and the doc says so.
- **`prose`** first flagged any block whose lines are mostly ≥ 60 characters. That removed Whitman and a Scots song in long metre: about 2 in 10 were false positives. Wrapped prose breaks mid-sentence, and long verse lines end in punctuation. The rule now also requires < 40 % of lines to end in punctuation, and the next 20 removals were all prose: biographies, a note to Scott, Ovid in prose, Aristotle's *Poetics*, Gutenberg licence clauses.

**Task 2 (verse).** Exact dedup removes 0 poems. MinHash with k = 5 words and (b, r) = (13, 9) (K = 117) finds **12 multi-document clusters (25 poems)**, which leaves 5 602 poems. Every inspected cluster is the same poem in two anthologies, as two editions with J between 0.73 and 0.95: Scott's songs both in *The Lady of the Lake* and in a Scottish anthology, Waller's "Go, Lovely Rose" and Fletcher's "Aspatia's Song" in Greenaway's anthology, Whitman in a historical anthology. No inspected cluster merged different poems.

*(b, r) exercise.* Requirement: P ≥ 0.95 at J = 0.85 and ≤ 0.05 at J = 0.5, with br ≤ 128. Seven designs qualify, the cheapest being (10, 8). I use (13, 9): P = 0.968 and 0.025, threshold ≈ 0.75. FineWeb's own (14, 8) narrowly fails the second bound (0.053).

**Task 3: the S-curve** (4 000 pairs of 200 random words, (13, 9)). The MinHash estimate of J has mean |Ĵ − J| = 0.021 and a bias of −0.0004. The empirical candidate rate per J bin matches Proposition 3. The largest gap over 20 bins is 2.5 standard errors, about the expected maximum of 20 near-normal deviations. So (ax + c) mod p is close enough to min-wise independent here.

**Task 4: contamination.**
- *FineWeb-Edu (20 000 documents, 15.2 M shingles):* one duplicate pair. The contamination is 0.00 % under both splits, because FineWeb already deduplicates with MinHash per crawl. That is a result, not a bug: dedup before the split mattered for the raw web, not for this sample.
- *Verse:* the split is a function of the cluster id. 5 311 train and 291 validation poems; no near-copy crosses the split by construction.

**Task 5: tokenisation and loading** (10 MB of FineWeb text):

|---|---|---|

| pretoken cache | 6.64 | hit rate 95.4 % |

Token files: verse train 2.53 M tokens (6.9 MB of text; 2.7 bytes per token, against 4.3 on FineWeb prose: the tokeniser was trained on prose). Validation is 137 k tokens. `Batches` delivers ~14 000 batches/s (230 M tokens/s) with pinned memory, against ~175 k tokens/s consumed by C1's compiled Tier 1 step: over 1 000× headroom. C1's `Trainer` trains on `--tokfile data/verse` unchanged.

**Bugs this module found.**
1. *Licence text in the corpus.* `gutenberg_body` matched `*** END OF THE PROJECT GUTENBERG EBOOK` only with a space after the asterisks, and only in upper case. 4 of 40 books use `***END OF…` or the old `End of the Project Gutenberg EBook`, so their licence text became "poems". The markers are now tolerant: 39 of 40 match, and the 40th was a cached 404 page from a hand-built URL. Downloads now always go through gutendex's own links.
2. *Workers that import torch.* On Windows (spawn), each pool worker re-imports the module of its function and the main module. With the worker function in a script that imports `lm.data.corpus`, which imported torch, every worker paid torch's import. The pool was 25 % *slower* than one process (5.0 against 6.6 MB/s). With the workers in `lm.tokenizer.parallel` and torch imported lazily in `Batches`, it reaches 17.8 MB/s.
3. *Two copies of the pool.* B4's `prep.py` had its own; both now use `lm.tokenizer.parallel`.

**Known limitation (authors).** 2 079 poems (37 %) come from anthologies without per-poem attributions, and they are labelled "Various". 168 are still credited to an anthology's compiler. When a cluster is collapsed, an explicitly attributed copy wins over the longest (this fixes Burns being credited to Greenaway). D1's author control is only as good as these labels.

## Answers

**Which rule removed the most, and was it right?** `prose`, 26 % of the words. In its first form it was partly wrong (Whitman); after the punctuation refinement, 20 of 20 inspected removals were prose. `max_lines` removes the most words after it, and it is deliberately lossy.

**S-curve gap.** None that is significant (max 2.5 standard errors over 20 bins). Two traps on the way: the first comparison evaluated Proposition 3 at each bin's mean J, which is biased on the steep, convex part of the curve. And it used the empirical binomial error, which is 0 in bins where all or no pairs are candidates. Both overstated the gap, up to "5.8 standard errors".

**Interview (60 s): deduplicate 10 TB, and know the threshold is right.**
1. Normalise a comparison key (NFKC, casefold, no punctuation) and drop exact duplicates by a 64-bit hash.
2. Shingle into word 5-grams. Compute MinHash signatures (~100 hashes) in a map step: embarrassingly parallel, CPU-bound, no shuffle.
3. Band the signatures. Emit (band, bucket) keys, shuffle by key, and emit star edges per bucket.
4. Connected components (distributed union–find), then keep one document per cluster.
5. Choose (b, r) from the S-curve: 1 − (1 − J^r)^b, with the threshold near (1/b)^{1/r}.

To know the threshold is right: measure the empirical S-curve on pairs with known J, inspect random clusters by eye, and check what the downstream metric does (validation contamination, memorisation). Split by cluster, never by document. Deduplicating each crawl separately can beat global dedup (FineWeb).

## Research-engineering tricks

- **Look at what a rule removes.** `--show <rule>` prints 20 removals. It caught a 20 % false-positive rate in the prose rule and the long poems that `max_lines` drops on purpose.
- **Provenance travels with the data.** Every record carries its source, book, licence and the book-level author, which made the Greenaway attribution bug fixable after the fact.
- **Cache every download, and keep the cache honest.** A second run downloads nothing. A cached error page is a permanent bug, so fetch only from the API's own links.
- **Deterministic hashes.** crc32 for words, blake2b for keys. Python's `hash()` is salted per process, so workers would disagree.
- **Polynomial hashing in uint64 modulo a Mersenne prime.** Every product of two residues stays below 2⁶², so there is no overflow and no Python loop over shingles.
- **Signatures in slices.** `MinHasher.signature` takes the minimum over blocks of 8 192 shingles, so memory stays at 8·K·block bytes for any document length.
- **Bands as opaque bytes.** `view(np.void)` turns each row of a band into one hashable value. `np.unique` gives the buckets, and star edges (to the first member) give O(n) edges, not O(n²).
- **Split by a hash of the cluster id.** The split is a pure function of (cluster, seed): reproducible, and constant on clusters by construction.
- **Null-hypothesis error bars.** Test a curve with the standard error of the prediction, and average the prediction over the points in a bin.
- **Light worker modules.** Under spawn, a worker imports the module of its function and the main module. Keep heavy imports (torch) out of both.
- **Streaming, ordered parallelism.** `Pool.imap` feeds `write_tokens` as results arrive, in document order. Nothing holds the corpus in memory.
- **Atomic writes.** `write_tokens` writes to `.tmp` names, then renames. A crash never leaves a truncated `.bin` under the real name.
- **Epoch tiling, not random offsets.** A random offset, then a permutation of non-overlapping windows: every token once per epoch. With i.i.d. starts, 37 % of tokens are never seen in one nominal epoch (Proposition 4).
