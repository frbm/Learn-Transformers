## Results

**Tests:** 13 pass (6 given, 7 extra) in 3 s on the CPU.

**All curvature numbers below come from smoke runs.** The full runs are left for the user.

**Task 4** (smoke run: 2 layers, width 128, RoPE, 512 windows × 128 tokens, P = 2.36 M, 8 chunks of 64 sequences):

| η | 2/η | λ_max at step 0 | step 20 | step 40 | loss 0 → 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.4 | 5.0 | 8.73 | 8.10 | 5.87 | 9.73 → 7.84 |

At η = 0.4 the initial sharpness is already above 2/η. GD does not diverge: λ_max falls towards 2/η within 40 steps (the self-stabilisation picture of §4, seen from above). One full-batch step (65k tokens) takes 0.15 s; one Lanczos with k = 20 (20 HVPs × 8 chunks) takes 12 s, with a peak of 4.3 GiB. The default learning rates (0.1, 0.2, 0.3; thresholds 20, 10 and 6.7) start below 2/η, so progressive sharpening has room to show.

## Answers

**Task 4 prediction (η halved mid-run).** The threshold 2/η doubles. The oscillation along the top eigenvector stops at once, and the loss decreases monotonically for a while. λ_max stops being pinned. Progressive sharpening resumes, and λ_max climbs until it reaches the new threshold 4/η₀. Then it hovers there again. The halving releases the ceiling, but it does not reset the sharpness: right after the change λ_max is still ≈ 2/η₀.

**Task 5 hypothesis (Muon).** Muon's step is −η·polar(M), a step of fixed spectral norm. There is no diagonal preconditioner, so λ_max(P⁻¹H) is undefined, and raw λ_max(H) against 2/η compares the wrong units. My hypothesis: the stability quantity is the curvature along the step actually taken. Take a quadratic model along u = Δθ. The loss falls iff uᵀHu < 2(−gᵀu). So the ratio r = uᵀHu / (−gᵀu) has the threshold 2 for every optimiser. For GD, r = η gᵀHg / ‖g‖² ≤ ηλ_max, which is the classical 2/η condition. For a hidden matrix under Muon, with ‖u‖₂ = η√(d_out/d_in), the dual statement is: the spectral-norm sharpness sup_{‖U‖₂ ≤ 1} UᵀHU must stay below 2‖G‖_*/η. I predict that r climbs to about 2 and hovers there under Muon, as λ_max(P⁻¹H) does near 38/η under Adam. The probe logs r, uᵀHu and gᵀu for both optimisers. r uses the gradient on the curvature batch. It is noisy, and it can be negative when the training-batch step is not a descent direction on the curvature batch.

**Interview (60 s).** The Hessian is the full second derivative of the loss. The GGN is JᵀΛJ: the curvature of the loss after the network is linearised, with Λ the logit Hessian. The Fisher is the expected outer product of score vectors under the model's own labels. For softmax cross-entropy, Λ = diag(p) − ppᵀ does not depend on the label. So GGN = Fisher exactly, and both are PSD. The Hessian is the GGN plus a residual term Σ(p − e_y)_k ∇²f_k, which is indefinite. They coincide when that term vanishes: a model linear in θ, or a perfect fit. The empirical Fisher (data labels) equals neither unless the model's conditionals equal the data's, and Adam's v is a diagonal of the empirical Fisher, so Adam is not a natural-gradient method.

## Research-engineering tricks

- **Dense oracles on a 173-parameter net.** Every matrix-free product is checked against `torch.autograd.functional.hessian` or a dense JᵀΛJ in float64.
- **Reuse the graph at a fixed θ.** A measurement makes about 35 matvecs at the same θ. `Curvature(cache=True)` keeps, per chunk, the graph of the gradient (an HVP is then one backward of ⟨g, v⟩) and the graph of the logits with Jᵀu for a dummy u (a GGN product is then two backward passes, no forward). Measured: 2.2 s instead of 3.3 s per measurement in the smoke run.
- **The double-backward trick for JVPs.** Jv = ∂⟨Jᵀu, v⟩/∂u needs only reverse mode, so it reuses the graph that already exists. `torch.func.linearize` would also cache the JVP, but it re-traces the model with `make_fx` at every θ. Here that made the GGN eigenvalues 3× **slower**. Measure before you adopt the "right" tool.
- **Cache keys include the version counter.** `theta.sub_(...)` keeps the tensor's identity but changes its values. A cache keyed on `id(theta)` alone serves stale graphs. The key is `(id(theta), theta._version)`, and a test updates θ in place to check it.
- **Caches cost memory, and on Windows an overflow is silent.** With 8 large chunks cached, Task 4 did not finish in 110 s instead of taking 12 s. The WDDM driver does not raise an out-of-memory error: it spills to system RAM. So caching is opt-in, on for the probe's small batch and off for the full-batch run. Log the peak memory of every run.
- **Two code paths.** SDPA (math backend) and naive attention give the same HVP and GGN product. Chunked products equal full-batch products. Cached products equal recomputed ones.
- **`autograd.grad`, never `.backward()`.** Curvature never touches `.grad` or the optimiser state. A test asserts that every `.grad` is still `None`.
- **The probe is a `Trainer` callback.** It measures in `before_step`, then adds uᵀHu along the step actually taken in `after_step`, at the old θ, without restoring the model. The `Trainer` closes its timing window first, so training time excludes the probe.
- **Math SDPA backend for double backward and forward-mode AD.** Flash and memory-efficient kernels support neither.
- **fp32 for curvature, bf16 for training.** HVPs in bf16 are noise. `upcast` keeps float64 as float64 (`.float()` would silently downcast the oracle tests).
- **Λu in closed form.** `p*u − p<p,u>` costs O(K) per row. The K × K matrix is never built.
- **Full re-orthogonalisation, twice, as matmuls.** Without it, Lanczos makes ghost copies of λ_max (`test_lanczos_without_reorth_makes_ghosts`). Lanczos vectors are stored in fp32 on the GPU: k × P × 4 bytes is 290 MB at Tier 0 with k = 10.
- **Symmetric preconditioning.** P^{-1/2} H P^{-1/2} has the spectrum of P⁻¹H and keeps Lanczos valid. A test checks it against the dense eigenvalues.
- **Ratio of sums over chunks.** Chunk weights n_chunk / N make the chunked loss, gradient, Hv and Gv exact, also when the last chunk is short.
- **One host sync per Hutchinson estimate.** The probe sum accumulates in float64 on the device. One `.item()` per probe would be one host–device sync per probe.
- **Full-batch GD without an optimiser.** `theta.sub_(g, alpha=eta)` on the flat vector. There is no hidden momentum, no weight decay and no state.
