## Results

- Training 16k merges on 4.13 MB takes **1.3 s** (target: 300 s). Encoding the corpus takes 0.57 s (target: 10 s).
- The `lru_cache` hit rate is 96.8 % (879k hits, 29k distinct chunks). Zipf: a small number of chunk types cover most of the tokens.

| tokeniser | EN B/tok | EN tok/word | FR B/tok | FR tok/word |

Our 16k tokeniser loses the most from EN to FR (−41 % B/tok). It saw no French. In French, accented letters take 2 bytes and its merges are English, so French words fall back to short pieces. o200k loses the least, because it has the most multilingual merges.

Task 6 (unigram φ fitted on canonical train encodings, test = last 10 %): canonical 2.2381 bpb, total over the fibre 2.2247 bpb, **gap 0.013 bpb**. Viterbi differs from BPE on 1.0 % of chunks. One EM step raises the log-likelihood (Proposition 4).

## On-paper answers

**Task 1.** `'s|'t|…` captures English contractions. ` ?\p{L}+` is a word with at most one leading space. ` ?\p{N}+` is a number. ` ?[^\s\p{L}\p{N}]+` is a punctuation run. `\s+(?!\S)` is a whitespace run *without its last space*, so that the last space attaches to the next word (`"   world"` → `"  "`, `" world"`). `\s+` takes the trailing whitespace at the end of the text. `l'homme` → `l`, `'`, `homme`: the French elision is not in the contraction list. `don't` → `don`, `'t`.

**§2 exercise.** The merges are (b,c), (a,b), (c,d), in rank order. BPE applies (b,c) first: `a·bc·d`. After that, no pair of the list is present, so the result is 3 tokens. `ab·cd` is 2 tokens. A shortest-segmentation encoder is a shortest path on the lattice of §3: a DP of cost O(n·L_max) per chunk. This is `viterbi` with all log φ equal.

**Task 3b design question.** User text must not become `<|endoftext|>` by default. If it does, a user can inject a document boundary or a control marker into the prompt. tiktoken refuses unless the caller allows it. Here `Tokenizer.encode` always splits specials, so call it only on trusted text. For user text, use a tokeniser with no specials.

## Research-engineering tricks

- **Slow oracle, fast implementation.** `reference_bpe` defines the semantics. The incremental trainer is tested against it, also on runs such as `aaaa`, where overlapping counts are a trap.
- **Count chunk types once.** Zipf: W ≈ 29k distinct chunks against 900k chunk tokens. All later work is per type, times its frequency.
- **Incremental updates with an inverted index.** `where[pair]` holds the chunks that contain the pair, so a merge touches only those. Update the counts by the diff of the old and new pair counters.
- **Lazy-deletion heap.** Push `(-count, pair)` on each change. When you pop, skip stale entries. The tuple order gives the tie rule.
- **`lru_cache` per instance.** Wrap a bound method in `__init__`, so that two tokenisers never share entries.
- **`regex.split` with one capturing group** puts the special tokens at the odd indices. One pass, no manual scan.
- **Decode to `bytes`.** Convert to `str` only once, at the end. A token can end in the middle of a UTF-8 character.
- **Property-based tests** (hypothesis) find the Unicode edge cases you do not think of.
- **Log space everywhere** on the lattice (`logaddexp`). Never multiply raw probabilities.
