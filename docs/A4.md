| Δ | 1 | 10 | 100 | 1 000 | 10⁴ | 10⁵ |

Deviations. Near Δ = 1, the fast planes have Δθ_j ≈ 1, not ≪ 1. Their cosines are cos(1) ≈ 0.54, not 1, so the exact value is below 64. Beyond Δ = b, all planes have dephased. The sum is a sum of almost random cosines that oscillates around 0, but the heuristic is linear in ln Δ and goes negative without bound. The heuristic is valid only for 1 ≪ Δ ≪ b.

## Answers

**Non-persistent buffers (Task 1).** The cos/sin tables are a function of `(d_h, max_seq_len, rope_base, rope_scaling)`, not learned. If they were saved, `load_state_dict` would overwrite the tables built for the new scaling with the training-time tables. It would also fail on shape when `max_seq_len` changes. Non-persistent tables are rebuilt from the config, so `eval_a4.py` builds a 2048-position model with PI/NTK/YaRN and loads the same checkpoint. `test_rope_buffers_not_in_checkpoint` tests this.

**Task 4 predictions.** Plain RoPE explodes after 512: its slow planes (r_j < 1 turn) see angles they never saw (Proposition 2). PI is worse *before* 512: it slows down every plane by 4, so neighbouring tokens become harder to tell apart, and the model was not fine-tuned on that. NTK and YaRN keep the fast planes and stay close to plain RoPE before 512, and degrade more slowly after. YaRN is the best of the three after 512. NoPE has no slow planes to break, so it degrades smoothly after 512. It is somewhat worse than RoPE at T ≤ 512.

**Interview (60 s).** Ask for ⟨R_m q, R_n k⟩ to depend only on n − m. Then R_mᵀR_n = R_{n−m}, so m ↦ R_m is a homomorphism from (Z, +) to O(d), so R_m = R_1^m. The spectral theorem block-diagonalises R_1 into 2×2 rotations: RoPE, with free frequencies θ_j. A model trained at 4k breaks at 32k because the slow planes did not make a full turn in 4k positions. Beyond 4k, they produce angles that were never seen. PI, NTK and YaRN rescale exactly those frequencies.

## Research-engineering tricks

- **Angle tables in float64**, then cos/sin, then cast. At position 131 071, fp32 angles are wrong by about 1e-2 rad. The test catches it.
- **Non-persistent buffers** for anything derived from the config.
- **The cache offset is the same in the mask and in the rotation.** One test covers prefill, a chunk and single steps with RoPE on.
- **Normalise, then rotate.** The rotation keeps the norm, so the QK-norm logit bound stays.
- **YaRN temperature in the attention scale** (`mscale² / √d_h`). It does not modify the cos/sin tables, so `apply_rope` stays a pure rotation and the norm test stays valid.
- **Test the formula and the code path together.** The Monte Carlo of Task 5 goes through `apply_rope`, not through a second copy of the formula.
